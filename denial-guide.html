Why claims get denied, and how to prevent it

A practical guide for practice managers and billing teams.

Most denials are avoidable. They usually trace back to a small set of causes at the front desk, in documentation, in coding or in follow-up. Below are the most common ones, with what to check for each. Rules differ by payer, so treat this as a checklist, not a rulebook.

1. Eligibility and registration errors

Wrong subscriber ID, inactive coverage, misspelled names, wrong date of birth or the wrong payer on file. Prevent it: verify eligibility before every visit, scan the insurance card at each visit, and match the demographics to the card exactly.

2. Missing or invalid prior authorization or referral

The service needed an authorization that was never obtained, expired or didn’t match the code billed. Prevent it: keep a payer-by-payer list of services that need authorization, record the authorization number and dates, and check them when the charge is entered.

3. Medical necessity gaps

The diagnosis doesn’t support the procedure under the payer’s policy, or the note doesn’t show why the service was needed. Prevent it: check Medicare LCD/NCD and payer policies for high-volume services, and ask providers to document the reason clearly.

4. Coding errors and weak documentation

Unsupported E/M levels, missing specificity in ICD-10-CM codes, missed laterality, or a code the note doesn’t support. Prevent it: code from the documentation, use provider queries when the note is unclear, and run regular coding audits so error patterns come back to coders.

5. Modifier and NCCI edit problems

Codes billed together that NCCI bundles, a missing modifier such as 25 or 59, or a modifier used without separate documentation. Prevent it: run NCCI edits in your claim scrubber, and only use a bypass modifier when the chart shows a distinct, separate service.

6. Duplicate claims

The same service is submitted twice, or a correction is sent as a new claim. Prevent it: track claim status before resubmitting, and use the payer’s corrected-claim process.

7. Timely filing and appeal deadlines

The claim or appeal is sent after the payer’s deadline, which is often hard to recover. Prevent it: work claims by age, set alerts before each payer’s limit, and keep proof of submission.

8. Coordination of benefits

The primary and secondary payers are wrong or out of date. Prevent it: ask about other coverage at each visit and update the order when a patient reports a change.

9. Incomplete claim data

Missing provider identifiers, place of service errors, or missing information the payer requires. Prevent it: use a claim scrubber, and review rejections by cause so the fix happens upstream.

10. No root-cause tracking

Teams rework denials one by one but never fix the source, so the same denial returns. Prevent it: categorize each denial by cause, payer and provider, and review the top three causes every month.

A simple monthly routine

  1. Pull your denials. Export last month’s denials with the reason code and payer.
  2. Group them. Sort by cause and by payer.
  3. Pick the top three. Fix the process behind each one, not just the claims.
  4. Measure. Track first-pass acceptance and denial rate next month.
